VIDEO:
Would Great Whites Survive Every Prehistoric Ocean?



Use the finished VO as provided.



MAIN GOAL:
Fast, cinematic, action-first. The great white should feel like it is being tested through a gauntlet of prehistoric oceans.



REFERENCE:
Use the same overall editing approach that worked on the primate video. Study Fossil for pacing, chapters, tier mechanics, visual resets, and music changes — but do not copy exact graphics, wording, jokes, music, or sequences.

====================
OPENING
====================



No logo intro or slow setup.



Within the first 20–25 sec:
- great white already swimming
- Dunkleosteus rising
- lunge
- shark dodges
- THEN reveal the six-ocean premise



Action first. Explanation second.



====================
PACING / RETENTION
====================



Aim for a meaningful visual reset every 20–40 sec:



- attack
- chase
- prey
- predator reveal
- escape
- environmental problem
- size comparison
- chapter
- tier update
- music shift



Do NOT freeze an active scene for a long fact explanation.



If a predator appears, show it DO something first, then explain why it matters.



Avoid long static AI shots or random prehistoric B-roll.



====================
IMPORTANT SET PIECES
====================



DEVONIAN:
Dunkleosteus rises → lunges → misses → shark circles back → oxygen/dead-zone threat



TRIASSIC:
shark slows → overheats → dives → oxygen gets worse → dies from the environment



JURASSIC:
great white hits ichthyosaur → blood spreads → Pliosaurus takes the kill



CRETACEOUS:
great white vs Cretoxyrhina → both scarred → split carcass → Tylosaurus hits → sharks scatter



EOCENE:
great white gets whale-calf kill → Basilosaurus appears → great white still thrives



PLIOCENE:
great white feeding → huge shadow → leaves instantly → Megalodon rises → reveal that great whites actually lived alongside it



====================
VISUAL STYLE
====================



Keep the great white as the recurring protagonist.



Use:
- animal close-ups
- tracking shots
- silhouettes
- size comparisons
- strong predator reveals
- clear environmental changes



Each ocean should feel visually different.



Do not exaggerate animal aggression or behavior beyond what the VO supports.



====================
MUSIC
====================



Change feel by chapter:



Devonian = mysterious/tension
Triassic = hostile/oppressive
Jurassic = predatory
Cretaceous = aggressive/chaotic
Eocene = confident/calm
Pliocene = darkest/biggest/cinematic



Use music drops for predator reveals, verdicts, and tier placements.



====================
ENDING
====================



After:
“It never had to beat megalodon.
It just had to outlast it.”



Show the completed tier board.



Then:



“So what do you think — did we get it right?



Because if these oceans prove anything, it’s that survival was never just about being the biggest thing in the water. It was about adapting when the water changed.”



Slow the pacing slightly here and make it cinematic.



MOST IMPORTANT:

1. Fast opening
